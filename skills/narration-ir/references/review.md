# Review the explanation

Cover all future beats. At each sentence, answer these questions using only the
source and information introduced so far. Record the affected beat, evidence,
defect, repair and remaining uncertainty, not a general score.

1. What precise claim does the speaker make? Is it supported by the cited source,
   or only reported by a speaker? Does a picture imply more than those words?
2. Can a newcomer point to the object being referenced? Has the term been defined
   before use, including within this sentence? Does the depicted actor know the
   answer, or does only the narrator know it?
3. What changes on screen? Can the viewer infer the intended relationship from
   the object and operation rather than reading a second paragraph of narration?
4. Does the unchanged picture retain its identity and state through the next
   sentence? Does a colour or shape suddenly acquire a different meaning?
5. Is the cue an onset, a midpoint emphasis, or a settled result? Does its lead-in
   reveal information prematurely? Is the hold long enough at the intended size?
6. Can the final payoff answer the opening question using what was actually
   shown? Are the sections steps in that explanation or unrelated topic labels?

For interviews, inspect qualification, uncertainty, negation and whose account
is being presented. For software, distinguish a proposed operation from an
observed run. For research, distinguish an example, general claim and measurement.
Keep disputed speech and interpretation as issues until evidence resolves them.

For finished output, watch with audio, inspect mid-transition frames and settled
holds at delivery size and a small player, and read captions at their actual pace.
Review a whole pass for chronology after local repairs. Report exactly what was
viewed and heard. Structural success and an independent review still do not
establish viewer understanding; that needs passage-specific viewer questions.

When comparing with script-only prompting, keep model, renderer, sources and
effort comparable. Record omitted meanings, false implications, selected timing
errors and revision work. Extra tokens and reviews can improve output regardless
of the IR; report those costs rather than attributing all improvement to the format.
